Stylized rock

A rock as a sculptor blocks it out for a stylized game: flat facets, soft bright edges, a few cracks, a few pits. Here nobody sculpts it: a graph makes it, and a seed makes another one. The tutorial ends as a generator: a mini planet of rocks, no two alike.

Before you start

You need Blender 5 and the Base Node add-on, version 0.12.1 or later. Steps 1 and 2 use the free standard library alone. From step 3 on you need the Smart Materials pack: it carries the three modules that give the rock its look (mesh_maps, generators, mesh_effects). The steps below are free; those modules are not.

ModuleTierWhat it gives here
mathfreefibonacci_point and random_direction: where the facets go.
mesh_mapspackThe curvature of the mesh, computed live and stored on it: where its edges and its hollows are.
generatorspackcurvature_mask: that curvature as two masks for the material, edges and cavities.
mesh_effectspackdents and chip_edges: pits and chips made in the mesh, stored for the material.

Every block below is a complete .bn file, its code beside the nodes it builds. Two ways to try a step:

  • Drag it. Drag the file icon of its block, or its title, onto empty space in Blender's 3D Viewport, and keep Instantiate the Example: a new object carries stylized_rock at the 3D cursor. A click on the file icon downloads the file instead.
  • Paste it. Press the copy button of its block, open a Geometry Node editor, press Ctrl+Shift+V and pick Add as Modifier, on any mesh object: the graph makes its own rock.

The pictures are rendered from these exact files by render.py.

What makes the look

Four things, in the order a sculptor does them:

  • Flat facets. The rock is cut by planes, as with a knife. No noise: noise makes lumps, not facets.
  • Soft edges. Where two facets meet, the edge is rounded a little, and it is paler than the faces.
  • A few large accidents. Two or three cracks, a few pits. Few, and large enough to read from far away.
  • One colour. The material is one grey, lit on the edges and darkened in the hollows. The shape does the work.

The first three are geometry, the last is the material, and the material reads what the geometry stored: that is what the pack is for.

1. A block of facets

A facet is a plane that touches a sphere. Take 50 points on the unit sphere, cut along the plane tangent at each: what is left is a convex block of 50 flat facets. Geometry Nodes has no node that cuts by planes, but it has the two that give the same block:

step1_block.bn: rock_block, stylized_rock

  1. Planes. fibonacci_point spreads the points evenly, random_direction pushes each one about: jitter 0 gives facets of one size, 1 large ones beside slivers. ConvexHull joins the points in triangles.
  2. Corners. Each triangle of the hull is three planes that meet: one corner of the block. That corner lies along the triangle's normal n, at n / (n ⋅ Position()). DualMesh turns the hull inside out, a point a triangle and a face a point: its faces are the facets.
  3. An egg. Dividing by 1 + z * taper is a perspective: the top shrinks, the bottom grows, and a plane stays a plane. Then size scales it.

Try: facets at 12, then 200; jitter at 0; taper at 0 and 0.6.

2. Clay

Fifty faces cannot carry a crack. A sculptor remeshes: the same shape, made of small even faces. MeshToSDFGrid then GridToMesh do it, voxel wide. A few passes of BlurAttribute on the positions then round the edges, and only them: a flat face stays where it is.

step2_clay.bn: clay, stylized_rock

  • voxel is the size of the finest detail to come: 6 mm on a rock 1 m tall, about 100 000 faces, two seconds to build.
  • soften is the width of the rounded edge, in passes: each one reaches a voxel further.

3. The edges, from the mesh maps

The pale edge is what makes a stylized rock read. A material cannot find the edges of a mesh by itself; the mesh can. One line at the end of the pipe, |> mesh_maps(…), stores its curvature, and curvature_mask gives it to the material as two masks:

step3_edges.bn: rock_stone, stylized_rock

  1. Edges. m.mask is 1 on what sticks out; Mix lays light over stone there.
  2. Hollows. m.cavities is 1 in what goes in; Mix lays dark over the layer before. Nothing is hollow yet: the next steps dig.
  • radius: 0.012 is the width the curvature is measured over, in metres: the width of the pale line. strength is its contrast.
  • mesh_maps stays the last line before the material in every step to come: it measures the mesh as it is when it gets it.

4. Cracks

A crack is a line across the rock, pushed in. The lines come from a Voronoi texture: the walls between its cells are planes, and where a plane crosses the rock it draws a line all around it, with forks where three cells meet. Three changes make cracks of them:

step4_cracks.bn: cracks, rock_stone, stylized_rock

  1. Lines. The texture is read at a place moved by a slow noise, bend far: the planes bend, the cracks curve. DISTANCE_TO_EDGE is the distance to the nearest wall.
  2. Open. A second slow noise opens the crack in patches and closes it at their border: amount 0 for none, 1 for cracks all around.
  3. Profile. x is 0 in the middle of the crack and 1 at its border. The groove is a smooth step of it, pushed in along the normal; a low lip rises just past the border, as clay does under a knife.

cracks stores how deep it cut as rock_crack, and the material darkens the hollows by it. The lips are edges: mesh_maps, after it in the pipe, finds them, and they turn pale with no line more.

Try: spacing at 0.4 for a rock in pieces; bend at 0 for straight cuts; amount at 1.

5. Pits and chips

Three lines of the pack finish the rock: two passes of dents, large and small, and chip_edges, which knocks bits off the edges. They are subtle on purpose: a pit deeper than a centimetre reads as a hole, not as stone.

step5_wear.bn: stylized_rock, rock_stone

  • The order. What changes the shape comes first, mesh_maps last: the maps see the cracks, the pits and the chips.
  • e = mesh_effects_read() gives the material what the effects stored: e.dent darkens a pit a little, e.chip lights a chip.
  • One seed. stylized_rock gives its seed to the block, the cracks, the pits and the chips: one number, one rock.

6. A mini planet of rocks, a seed each

One graph, one seed, one rock: it is a generator. To cover a ground with rocks that differ, instancing is no help: instances are copies of one geometry. A foreach zone runs its body once a point, with that point's values: each turn builds a rock of its own.

step6_planet.bn: rock_planet, stylized_rock

  1. Planet. An IcoSphere with low hills, and points on it by Poisson disk, spacing apart: no two rocks in the same place.
  2. Rocks. The zone reads each point's place, its normal up and its index i. seed + i is the seed of its rock; two random vectors give its proportions, its size and its turn.
  3. Standing. AlignRotationToVector points the rock's top along the normal of the ground; RotateRotation, in LOCAL space, then leans it and turns it about that normal. It is sunk a third into the ground.
  • stylized_rock takes two inputs more here: size, and voxel, divided by the rock's scale: once scaled, every rock has faces of the same size, and a small rock fewer of them.
  • Every rock is real geometry: about 650 000 faces for the planet, some ten seconds to build. Raise voxel while you tune, lower it to render.
  • The sizes of the cracks and the pits are in the rock's own metres: a rock scaled down has finer ones.

Try: change seed: another planet. spacing at 0.6 crowds it. Replace the IcoSphere with a Grid, or with your own terrain: the rest stays as it is.
